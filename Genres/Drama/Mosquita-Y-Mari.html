Mosquita y Mari (2012)

About the Film

A Sundance Coming-of-Age Drama from East Los Angeles

Mosquita y Mari is a 2012 coming-of-age drama written and directed by Aurora Guerrero in her feature film debut. It premiered at the 2012 Sundance Film Festival and was the first narrative feature by a Chicana director to screen there. The story follows Yolanda, nicknamed Mosquita and played by Fenessa Pineda, and Mari, played by Venecia Troncoso. The two Chicana high school students in East Los Angeles form a bond that confuses them at times, and the film explores identity, family, class and community. Joaquín Garrido, Laura Patalano, Dulce María Solís and Marisela Uscanga also appear. Guerrero funded the film through Kickstarter, and Wolfe Releasing opened it in US theaters on August 3, 2012. It runs 85 minutes and is not rated. The film won the Outstanding First U.S. Dramatic Feature Film Award at Outfest 2012, the Queer Award at the Torino GLBT Film Festival, and Pineda won Outfest’s Outstanding Actress Award. Because its leads are high school students, viewers should check content guidance before watching. For another LGBTQ+ coming-of-age story, see Blue Is the Warmest Color.
