The Reluctant Fundamentalist (2012)

About the Film

A Post-9/11 Political Thriller from Mohsin Hamid’s Novel

The Reluctant Fundamentalist is a 2012 political thriller drama directed by Mira Nair. William Wheeler wrote the screenplay, based on Mohsin Hamid’s 2007 novel. The story unfolds in a Lahore teahouse, where Changez Khan, a professor, meets Bobby Lincoln, an American journalist who suspects him of involvement in the kidnapping of an American colleague. As the conversation unfolds, Changez recounts his career in New York and how his life changed after the 9/11 attacks. The film premiered at the 2012 Venice International Film Festival, where it opened the festival, and it was co-financed by IFC Films and Cathay-Keris Films. Riz Ahmed plays Changez, Liev Schreiber plays Bobby Lincoln, Kate Hudson plays Erica, and Kiefer Sutherland plays Jim Cross. The film runs 130 minutes and is rated R for language, some violence and brief sexuality. For a similar drama, see My Fault or Ammonite.

In Lahore, Pakistani professor Changez Khan meets American journalist Bobby Lincoln for tea while student protests rage outside and a colleague has been kidnapped. Changez tells his story of building a career on Wall Street and how the events of 9/11 changed the way he is treated, and why he returned home.

The film was directed by Mira Nair. William Wheeler wrote the screenplay, based on Mohsin Hamid’s 2007 novel The Reluctant Fundamentalist.

Riz Ahmed plays Changez Khan, Kate Hudson plays Erica, Kiefer Sutherland plays Jim Cross, and Liev Schreiber plays Bobby Lincoln. Om Puri plays Changez’s father, Shabana Azmi plays his mother, and Adil Hussain plays Mustafa Fazil.

The film had its world premiere at the 2012 Venice International Film Festival, where it opened the festival on August 29, 2012. It opened in limited release in the United States on April 26, 2013.
