The Reader (2008)

About the Film

A Post-War Drama Adapted from Bernhard Schlink’s Novel

The Reader (Der Vorleser) is a 2008 drama directed by Stephen Daldry, with a screenplay by David Hare based on Bernhard Schlink’s 1995 German novel. In post-war West Germany, 15-year-old Michael Berg falls ill with scarlet fever and is helped home by Hanna Schmitz, a stranger twice his age. The two begin a secret affair. Years later, Michael, now a law student, sees Hanna on trial for Nazi war crimes and realizes that she is illiterate and has hidden it. Kate Winslet plays Hanna, Ralph Fiennes plays the adult Michael, and David Kross plays the younger Michael. Winslet won the Academy Award for Best Actress for the role. The film runs 124 minutes and is rated R for sexual content, nudity and language, so viewers should check content guidance before watching. For a similar drama, see My Fault or Ammonite.

Frequently Asked Questions

In post-war Germany, 15-year-old Michael Berg is helped home by Hanna Schmitz, a woman twice his age, after he falls ill. They begin a secret affair. Years later, as a law student, Michael sees Hanna on trial for Nazi war crimes and learns a secret about her past that changes how he understands their relationship.

The Reader was directed by Stephen Daldry. David Hare wrote the screenplay, based on Bernhard Schlink’s 1995 novel Der Vorleser.

Kate Winslet plays Hanna Schmitz, Ralph Fiennes plays the adult Michael Berg, and David Kross plays the young Michael Berg. Lena Olin plays Rose Mather and Bruno Ganz plays Professor Rohl.

The Reader opened in limited release in the United States on December 10, 2008, followed by a wider release in early 2009.

The film runs 124 minutes, or 2 hours and 4 minutes.

Yes. It is adapted from Bernhard Schlink’s German novel Der Vorleser, published in 1995.

Yes. Kate Winslet won the Academy Award for Best Actress for her role as Hanna Schmitz. The film earned five Academy Award nominations in total, including Best Picture and Best Director.

The Reader is rated R in the United States for sexual content, nudity and language. It deals with the Holocaust and Nazi war crimes, and it is not recommended for younger viewers.
