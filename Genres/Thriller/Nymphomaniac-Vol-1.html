Nymphomaniac: Vol. I (2013)

About the Film

A Story Told Through Memory

Nymphomaniac: Vol. I (2013) is the first installment of Lars von Trier’s two-part film. The story begins when Seligman discovers Joe, a woman who has been beaten and left in an alley. He brings her to his apartment, where she begins to recount episodes from her life. Her account moves through memories and encounters, while Seligman responds with observations, associations and reflections.

The film is structured as a series of chapters, using Joe’s recollections as the foundation for a wider exploration of identity, intimacy, shame, connection and the ways people interpret their own experiences. The framing conversations between Joe and Seligman give the film a distinctive narrative rhythm, moving between personal confession and philosophical discussion.

Charlotte Gainsbourg portrays Joe in the framing story, while Stacy Martin plays the younger Joe in the earlier-life sequences. Stellan Skarsgård appears as Seligman, with Shia LaBeouf, Christian Slater, Jamie Bell, Uma Thurman, Willem Dafoe, Mia Goth and other performers in supporting roles. Directed and written by Lars von Trier, Volume I establishes the narrative that continues in Nymphomaniac: Vol. II.
