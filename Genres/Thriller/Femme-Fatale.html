Femme Fatale (2002)

About the Film

A Heist, a New Identity and a Dangerous Past

Femme Fatale (2002) is a neo-noir crime thriller written and directed by Brian De Palma. During a high-profile event at the Cannes Film Festival, thief Laure Ash helps steal a valuable diamond collection before turning against her accomplices and fleeing with the prize. After encountering Lily, a woman who closely resembles her, Laure takes on a new identity and builds a different life. But when photographer Nicolas Bardo captures an image that connects her to her past, the people she betrayed begin closing in. Blending a jewel-heist premise with mistaken identity, suspense, visual misdirection and De Palma’s signature twists, the film explores reinvention and the consequences of deception.

Frequently Asked Questions

The film follows Laure Ash, a jewel thief who betrays her accomplices during a Cannes film-festival heist, assumes another woman’s identity and later faces exposure when a photographer recognizes her.

Femme Fatale was written and directed by Brian De Palma.

The film premiered in France in April 2002 and received its US theatrical release on November 6, 2002.

The principal cast includes Rebecca Romijn, Antonio Banderas, Peter Coyote, Eriq Ebouaney and Édouard Montoute.

Femme Fatale has a commonly listed runtime of approximately 114 minutes.

It is commonly classified as a thriller, crime and mystery film, with neo-noir and erotic-thriller elements.

Rebecca Romijn plays Laure Ash and the look-alike character Lily Watts.

Yes. The story’s jewel theft takes place during a Cannes Film Festival event, and the film itself screened at the 2002 festival.
