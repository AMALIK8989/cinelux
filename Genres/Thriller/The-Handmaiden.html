About the Film

A Deceptive Scheme. An Unexpected Connection.

The Handmaiden (2016), originally titled Ah-ga-ssi, is a South Korean mystery thriller directed by Park Chan-wook. Set during the Japanese occupation of Korea in the 1930s, the film follows Sook-hee, a young Korean pickpocket recruited by a conman known as Count Fujiwara. She is sent to the secluded estate of Lady Hideko, a Japanese heiress, to gain her trust and help carry out a plan to take her inheritance. As the scheme unfolds, concealed histories, shifting perspectives and unexpected emotions complicate the deception.

Frequently Asked Questions About The Handmaiden

Set in 1930s Korea during Japanese rule, the film follows Sook-hee, a pickpocket hired to serve Lady Hideko as part of a con designed to take the heiress’s fortune. The plan becomes complicated as secrets emerge and loyalties shift.

The Handmaiden was directed by South Korean filmmaker Park Chan-wook, who co-wrote the screenplay with Chung Seo-kyung.

Yes. The film is loosely adapted from Sarah Waters’s 2002 historical crime novel Fingersmith, relocating the story from Victorian Britain to 1930s Korea.

The principal cast includes Kim Min-hee as Lady Hideko, Kim Tae-ri as Sook-hee, Ha Jung-woo as Count Fujiwara, Cho Jin-woong as Uncle Kouzuki, Kim Hae-sook and Moon So-ri.

The film premiered in competition at the 2016 Cannes Film Festival on May 14, 2016, and opened in South Korea on June 1, 2016.

The theatrical version runs approximately 144 to 145 minutes. A separate extended edition runs approximately 167 to 168 minutes, depending on the listing.

The film is primarily in Korean and Japanese. Language presentation may vary by release; check the selected version for subtitle options.

The Handmaiden blends mystery, thriller, period drama and romance, using an intricate con story and shifting perspectives to build suspense.
