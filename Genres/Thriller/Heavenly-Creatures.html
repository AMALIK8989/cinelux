Heavenly Creatures (1994)

About the Film

A Friendship Between Fantasy and Reality

Directed by Peter Jackson and co-written with Fran Walsh, Heavenly Creatures is a psychological crime drama inspired by the notorious Parker-Hulme case in New Zealand. Set in Christchurch during the 1950s, the film follows Pauline Parker and Juliet Hulme, two teenagers from different backgrounds who discover a powerful connection through their shared imagination, creative interests and fascination with fantasy.

Pauline and Juliet build an elaborate imaginary kingdom called Borovnia, creating a private world that becomes increasingly important to their lives. Their friendship grows into an intense emotional attachment, while their families become concerned about the relationship and the girls' increasingly isolated behaviour.

As the possibility of separation becomes real, the fantasy world they have built collides with the outside world. The film gradually moves from an imaginative coming-of-age story into a disturbing psychological crime drama, exploring obsession, adolescence, family conflict and the consequences of an increasingly destructive bond.

Featuring early leading performances from Melanie Lynskey and Kate Winslet, Heavenly Creatures is notable for its unusual visual imagination and its treatment of a real historical crime. The film received widespread critical acclaim and earned an Academy Award nomination for Best Original Screenplay.

Frequently Asked Questions

Heavenly Creatures follows Pauline Parker and Juliet Hulme, two teenagers in 1950s Christchurch who develop an intense friendship and create an elaborate imaginary world. When their families attempt to separate them, their relationship leads to a shocking crime.

Yes. The film is inspired by the Parker-Hulme murder case, which took place in Christchurch, New Zealand, in 1954. Pauline Parker and Juliet Hulme were involved in the murder of Pauline's mother, Honora Parker.

Peter Jackson directed Heavenly Creatures and co-wrote the screenplay with Fran Walsh.

Melanie Lynskey plays Pauline Parker, one of the two central characters in the film.

Kate Winslet portrays Juliet Hulme in her feature film debut.

Heavenly Creatures premiered in 1994. Its United States theatrical release was on November 16, 1994.

The commonly listed theatrical version runs approximately 99 minutes. Some official and alternate releases list longer versions.

Borovnia is the imaginary kingdom created by Pauline and Juliet. Their fantasy world becomes a central part of their friendship and their attempt to escape the pressures of everyday life.

The film takes place mainly in Christchurch, New Zealand, during the early 1950s.

Heavenly Creatures is generally classified as a psychological drama, crime thriller and biographical film, with coming-of-age and fantasy elements.

Yes. Peter Jackson and Fran Walsh received an Academy Award nomination for Best Original Screenplay for Heavenly Creatures.
