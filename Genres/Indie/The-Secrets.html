The Secrets (2007)

About the Film

An Israeli Love Story Behind Seminary Walls

The Secrets, known in Hebrew as Ha-Sodot, is a 2007 Israeli and French drama directed by Avi Nesher, who co-wrote the screenplay with Hadar Galron. Ania Bukstein plays Naomi, the devout daughter of a rabbi, who meets her secular roommate Michelle, played by Michal Shtamler, at a women’s seminary in Safed. Their friendship grows into a romance that the strict community cannot accept. The film also follows Anouk, played by Fanny Ardant, a mysterious woman with a past crime whom the students try to cleanse through religious rites. Nesher co-produced the film with David Silber. It had its world premiere on June 14, 2007, and it runs 127 minutes. Reviews have focused on its portrait of Orthodox Jewish life and the friendship at the film’s centre.

Frequently Asked Questions

At a Jewish women’s seminary in Safed, Israel, Naomi, the devout daughter of a rabbi, meets her secular roommate Michelle. The two become close, and romantic feelings develop between them. The story also follows Anouk, a mysterious woman with a past crime, whom the students try to purify through religious rites.

The Secrets was directed by Avi Nesher. He co-wrote the screenplay with Hadar Galron and co-produced the film with David Silber.

The film stars Ania Bukstein as Naomi and Michal Shtamler as Michelle. Fanny Ardant plays Anouk, and Dana Ivgy, Sefi Rivlin, Alma Zack, Adir Miller and Guri Alfi also appear in the cast.

The Secrets had its world premiere on June 14, 2007. It was released in the United States in a limited theatrical run on November 26, 2008.

The Secrets runs about 127 minutes (2 hours 7 minutes).

The Secrets is rated R in the United States. It deals with a romance between two women and with religious and sexual themes, so viewer discretion is advised.
