Jack the Giant Slayer (2013)

About the Film

A Giant-Sized Retelling of the Jack Fairy Tales

Jack the Giant Slayer is a 2013 American fantasy adventure film directed by Bryan Singer. The screenplay is by Darren Lemke, Christopher McQuarrie and Dan Studney, from a story by Lemke and David Dobkin, and it draws on the British fairy tales Jack the Giant Killer and Jack and the Beanstalk. Nicholas Hoult plays Jack, a farmhand who accidentally opens a gateway between his world and a race of giants, reigniting an ancient war. Eleanor Tomlinson plays Princess Isabelle, and Stanley Tucci plays Lord Roderick, the king’s advisor. Ian McShane, Bill Nighy, Ewan McGregor and Eddie Marsan also star. The film premiered in Hollywood on February 26, 2013, and Warner Bros. Pictures released it in US theaters on March 1, 2013. It runs 114 minutes and is rated PG-13. IMDb lists one win and four nominations. For another action-adventure, see Jurassic World.

Frequently Asked Questions

When young farmhand Jack unwittingly opens a portal between his realm and a race of giants, it rekindles an ancient war. Jack must face an army of foes he thought existed only in legend, and he may win the love of a brave princess, Isabelle.

Bryan Singer directed the film. The screenplay is by Darren Lemke, Christopher McQuarrie and Dan Studney, from a story by Lemke and David Dobkin.

Nicholas Hoult plays Jack and Eleanor Tomlinson plays Princess Isabelle. Stanley Tucci plays Lord Roderick, Ian McShane plays King Brahmwell, Bill Nighy plays General Fallon, Ewan McGregor plays Elmont, Eddie Marsan plays Crawe and Ewen Bremner plays Wicke. Ralph Brown, John Kassir, Cornell John, Christopher Fairbank, Simon Lowe, Richard Dixon, Joy McBrinn, Lee Boardman, Mingus Johnston, Raine McCormack, Sam Creed, Santi Scinelli, Jody Halse and Warwick Davis also have confirmed roles on this page.

The film premiered in Hollywood on February 26, 2013, and was released in US theaters on March 1, 2013, by Warner Bros. Pictures.

The film runs 114 minutes, or 1 hour and 54 minutes.

Yes. It draws on the British fairy tales Jack the Giant Killer and Jack and the Beanstalk. The film was earlier titled Jack the Giant Killer.

The film is rated PG-13 in the United States for fantasy action and violence. Parents are advised to check content guidance, and younger children may find the giant battles frightening.
