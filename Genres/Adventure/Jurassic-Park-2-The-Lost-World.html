The Lost World: Jurassic Park (1997)

About the Film

Back to the Dinosaurs’ Second Island

The Lost World: Jurassic Park is a 1997 American science fiction adventure directed by Steven Spielberg. It is the second film in the Jurassic Park series and is loosely based on Michael Crichton’s 1995 novel The Lost World. David Koepp wrote the screenplay. Jeff Goldblum returns as mathematician Ian Malcolm, who is drawn back to the dinosaurs’ second island with a team that includes paleontologist Sarah Harding, played by Julianne Moore, and big-game hunter Roland Tembo, played by Pete Postlethwaite. Richard Attenborough reprises John Hammond. The film’s prologue shows a young girl attacked on the island during a yacht cruise, and the story follows the teams that arrive to study or capture the dinosaurs. Its tone is darker than the original film. The film opened in Los Angeles on May 19, 1997, and was released in the United States on May 23, 1997, by Universal Pictures. It runs 129 minutes and is rated PG-13. John Williams composed the score, and it grossed about $618 million worldwide. For another dinosaur adventure, try Jurassic Park or Pirates of the Caribbean: On Stranger Tides.

Four years after the events on Isla Nublar, John Hammond’s company sends a team to a second island where the dinosaurs live on their own. Ian Malcolm, paleontologist Sarah Harding and hunter Roland Tembo are among those who travel there, and they must survive the dinosaurs.

Steven Spielberg directed the film. David Koepp wrote the screenplay, loosely based on Michael Crichton’s 1995 novel The Lost World.

Jeff Goldblum plays Dr. Ian Malcolm and Julianne Moore plays Sarah Harding. Pete Postlethwaite plays Roland Tembo, Arliss Howard plays Peter Ludlow and Richard Attenborough plays John Hammond. Vince Vaughn plays Nick Van Owen, Richard Schiff plays Eddie Carr and Peter Stormare plays Dieter Stark. Joseph Mazzello plays Tim Murphy, Ariana Richards plays Lex Murphy and Camilla Belle plays Cathy Bowman. The full cast list appears on this page.
