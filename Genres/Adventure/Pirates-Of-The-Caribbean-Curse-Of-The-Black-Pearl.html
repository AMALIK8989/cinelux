About the Film

The First Film in the Pirates of the Caribbean Franchise

Pirates of the Caribbean: The Curse of the Black Pearl is a 2003 American fantasy action-adventure film directed by Gore Verbinski. Ted Elliott and Terry Rossio wrote the screenplay from a story by Stuart Beattie, and the film is based on Walt Disney’s Pirates of the Caribbean theme park attraction. Johnny Depp plays Captain Jack Sparrow, who arrives at Port Royal without a ship or crew. Orlando Bloom plays blacksmith Will Turner, who allies with Sparrow to rescue Elizabeth Swann, played by Keira Knightley, from Captain Hector Barbossa, played by Geoffrey Rush, and the cursed crew of the Black Pearl. Jack Davenport, Jonathan Pryce, Kevin R. McNally, Zoe Saldaña and Mackenzie Crook also star. The film was released in US theaters on July 9, 2003, runs 143 minutes and is rated PG-13. It is the first film in the franchise, followed by Dead Man’s Chest (2006) and At World’s End (2007). For another adventure, see Jack the Giant Slayer.

Frequently Asked Questions

Captain Jack Sparrow arrives at Port Royal without a ship or crew. Blacksmith Will Turner allies with him to rescue Elizabeth Swann, who has been kidnapped by Captain Hector Barbossa and the cursed crew of the Black Pearl.

Gore Verbinski directed the film. Ted Elliott and Terry Rossio wrote the screenplay, from a story by Stuart Beattie.

Johnny Depp plays Captain Jack Sparrow and Orlando Bloom plays Will Turner. Keira Knightley plays Elizabeth Swann, Geoffrey Rush plays Captain Hector Barbossa and Jonathan Pryce plays Governor Weatherby Swann. Jack Davenport plays Commodore Norrington, Lee Arenberg plays Pintel and Mackenzie Crook plays Ragetti. Kevin R. McNally plays Joshamee Gibbs and Zoe Saldaña plays Anamaria. Treva Etienne plays Koehler, David Bailie plays Cotton, Trevor Goddard plays Grapple, Damian O’Hare plays Lt. Gillette, Giles New plays Murtogg and Michael Berry Jr. plays Twigg.

The film was released in US theaters on July 9, 2003, by Walt Disney Pictures.

The film runs 143 minutes, or 2 hours and 23 minutes.

Yes. It is based on Walt Disney’s Pirates of the Caribbean theme park attraction. It is the first film in the franchise, followed by Dead Man’s Chest (2006) and At World’s End (2007).
